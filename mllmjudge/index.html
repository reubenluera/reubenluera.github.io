MLLM as a UI Judge: Predicting Human Perception of User Interfaces

Summary: AI UX Research Report for Adobe Creative Cloud Apps

Link to full Paper

PROJECT SUMMARY

COMPANY

Adobe Research

ROLE

Lead Researcher / First Author

QUAL METHODS

Contextual Inq · RITE testing · Semi-structured interviews

QUANT METHODS

Likert scoring · Pairwise A/B · Avg Error · Model–human agreement · Data Viz

TIMELINE

Phase 1: 3 RITE rounds · Phase 2: benchmark study

STUDY SCALE

10 designers · 30 interfaces · 3 MLLMs · 9 UI factors

What happened
  • Delivered findings and a decision framework to Adobe stakeholders. When to use AI critique as a fast gut-check and when to escalate to people.
  • Submitted to UIST and started a patent process. A paper based on the work was submitted (submission is not acceptance); a related patent remains in process.
  • Benchmarked 3 MLLMs against human ratings. 10 designers, 30 interfaces, 9 UI factors. Agreement was strongest on clearly differentiated A/B options, so results are directional.

Research Methods

Qualitative and Quantitative

How do Designers use AI in Adobe Workflows for evaluation?

Two research questions drove the study. The qualitative phase asked how designers integrate AI feedback into their workflows, using contextual inquiry, three RITE rounds of usability testing on usertesting.com, personas, a customer journey map and an unmoderated survey. The quantitative phase asked whether multimodal LLMs produce human-like design feedback or hallucinate, by benchmarking three models against a human baseline through a pipeline I coded.

Research Methods Timeline

PHASE 1: Qualitative Research

Contextual inquiry with junior designers as they worked with AI, then personas, a customer journey map and a design report, then usability testing of the new in-product evaluative toggle on usertesting.com.

PHASE 2: Quantitative Research

People and AI rated the same designs. Comparing the two shows whether AI can stand in for human feedback at the early design stage, and where it cannot.

Qualitative Research

Contextual Inquiry Insights

Designers treat AI feedback as a fast gut-check, not a verdict.

Four themes came out of think-aloud transcripts across three RITE rounds with 10 Adobe Creative Cloud designers. Trust hinges on specificity: 8 of 10 acted on concrete callouts like "the CTA is below the fold" and tuned out generic advice, and vague critique eroded trust faster than no critique at all. Power users want model choice: 7 of 10, all daily AI users, wanted the toggle to expose model selection rather than abstract it away. Brand and audience context is the missing input: 4 of 10 re-entered brand voice and constraints by hand every round. Fatigue hits by round three: 8 of 10 stopped reading full responses and scanned for headers, and the toggle reduced context switching but did not fix late-iteration load.

Usability Testing

Prototype Testing:

Unmoderated usability sessions on usertesting.com with Adobe Creative Cloud designers. Each participant asked for AI feedback on a design through the in-app evaluative toggle, applied a change and re-evaluated. Think-aloud recordings tracked hesitation and trust in the AI output, and the RITE format let me change the prototype between rounds.

Deliverables

Persona: Understanding the Target User

The persona is a 21-year-old agency visual designer with one year of experience. He wants a neutral read on iterations without scheduling a formal review, uses AI to narrow 2 to 4 directions to one, and wants to move without waiting on senior availability or a research budget. His frustrations are the export, paste and read loop in a browser every round, a cycle that repeats 2 to 5 times per project, and feedback that feels generic without brand or audience context. Three takeaways: the primary user is the junior designer without a research budget, free and instant beats better but gated, and asking AI is lower friction than asking peers.

Customer Journey Map

The journey map follows a junior designer through five phases: design and iterate, export and context switch, paste into AI and get feedback, compare and choose a direction, then apply changes and repeat. Frustration peaks at the export step and again at the repeat step, because small tweaks do not feel worth a full round trip. Three takeaways: AI means quick feedback, the Adobe to chat to Adobe loop is the core friction, and that friction compounds with every iteration. The map was built for designer and PM stakeholders.

DESIGN RECOMMENDATIONS

Make feedback actionable

1. Keep feedback in the workflow

Keep critique inside the design tool so designers never have to leave their editing flow.

2. Anchor critique to the interface

Point to the exact interface region and pair each critique with a clear next action.

3. Adapt feedback across rounds

Start with full rationale, then shift to concise “what changed” summaries in later rounds.

Quantitative Research

Methods

Study Information (Human Ground Truth)

Thirty real-world designs from a professional design dataset were each rated by 500 unique people, for 15,000 raw responses. After quality filtering, 12,000 responses remained. Participants rated nine UI factors across cognitive, perceptual and emotional categories on a 1 to 7 Likert scale and completed A/B comparisons. This human benchmark is what the models were scored against.

Validating AI use for Design evaluation

Given a design, people rated it first. Then Claude 3.5, GPT-4o and LLaMA 3.2 rated the same design through the pipeline, and I measured where each model agreed or disagreed with the human scores. The question was whether an LLM is reliable enough for early-stage interface testing, and which one best simulates human preferences.

Quantitative Results

How close were the MLLMs to Human Scores?

Human target scores are the black line and each bar is a model. All three models moderately approximated human scores, with errors usually inside plus or minus one point on the 7-point scale. Models skewed more positive than people, and LLaMA diverged most on the Interesting factor.

MLLMs do increasingly well with A/B Testing

Accuracy on pairwise A/B comparisons rose with the size of the human score gap. Claude and GPT climbed from near chance on the closest pairs to roughly 90 percent on the clearest ones, while LLaMA stayed between 50 and 65 percent regardless. Models are reliable for clearly differentiated options and struggle on close calls.

Quantitative Study Takeaways

Major Learnings and Takeaways

MLLMs are useful as approximators, not judges. AI critique belongs inside the app rather than in a separate browser tab. Benchmarking against human data is how to fine tune internal models. And the approach fits early design stages best, where a fast directional read is worth more than precision.

Outcomes/Deliverables Summary

Produced an HCI research paper submitted to UIST, started a patent filing for the pipeline, and presented the design team with design suggestions. The team planned an evaluative agent in Adobe Creative Cloud based on this work.
